# Privacy policy source review

Reviewed 22 November 2026. This note gathers primary sources for updating the website’s privacy disclosures. It is a source summary, not a legal opinion. Vendor statements below describe their published documentation; they do not verify the site’s account settings, contract, or actual data flows.

## EU and Bulgarian sources

- Because the operator is established in Bulgaria, GDPR Article 3(1) covers processing carried out in the context of that establishment, regardless of where the processing itself takes place. Articles 12–14 set transparency requirements; Articles 15–22 describe rights, subject to their conditions; Article 77 gives a right to complain to a supervisory authority. Use the actual processing map to fill in controller contact, purposes and bases, recipients, transfers, retention criteria, and applicable rights. [GDPR, especially Articles 3, 4(7), 12–14, 15–22 and 77](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng)
- ePrivacy Directive Article 5(3) covers storing information on, or accessing information already stored on, a user’s terminal equipment. It requires clear information and consent, with exceptions for transmission and storage/access strictly necessary for a service explicitly requested by the user. Bulgaria’s current [Electronic Commerce Act, Article 4a](https://www.mi.government.bg/file/2026/05/zet_03_02_26.pdf) sets out clear GDPR Article 13 information and an opportunity to refuse, with transmission/requested-service exceptions. The [CPDP’s current cookie guidance](https://cpdp.bg/%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%B5%D1%86%D0%B8%D1%84%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%BD%D0%B8-%D0%BD%D0%B0%D1%81%D0%BE%D0%BA%D0%B8-%D0%B7%D0%B0-%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%BB%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%BD%D0%B8-%D1%81%D0%B5%D0%BA%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80/) describes prior consent for non-essential cookies/terminal access, except where strictly necessary. This difference in phrasing is material; for non-essential device storage/access, the regulator’s published pre-consent position is the cautious source to follow, and any reliance on the wording difference should be checked with Bulgarian counsel. [ePrivacy Directive, Article 5(3)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2002/58/art_5/par_3/oj/eng)
- In _Planet49_, the Court of Justice held that a pre-ticked checkbox is not valid consent for cookie storage/access, whether or not the stored information is personal data; users also need information about cookie duration and third-party access. [CJEU, C-673/17](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A62017CJ0673)
- The [Bulgarian Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA)](https://cpdp.bg/en/legislation/personal-data-protection-act/) says in Article 25c that, when consent is the basis for processing a child under 14 in connection with an information society service offered directly to them, consent must be given by a parent or guardian. The [CPDP complaint instructions](https://cpdp.bg/en/lodging-complaints-and-alerts/) identify the Commission for Personal Data Protection as the national complaint route and currently say complaints must be in Bulgarian; email submissions must be electronic documents signed with a qualified electronic signature. [PDPA Article 38](https://cpdp.bg/en/legislation/personal-data-protection-act/) sets a national filing window of six months from awareness, no later than two years after the alleged violation. A policy can identify the CPDP without reproducing procedural details that may change.
- GDPR lawful basis for personal-data processing and ePrivacy’s terminal-equipment rule are separate questions. A site’s consent choice for an optional browser beacon does not stop provider-side processing of requests needed to deliver or secure the site. This distinction follows from applying GDPR and ePrivacy to different processing operations; confirm it against live requests and account configuration. [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng), [ePrivacy Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2002/58/art_5/par_3/oj/eng)

## Configured infrastructure and communication providers

### Cloudflare: proxy, security, and Web Analytics

- Cloudflare says end-user traffic processed through its services can include IP addresses, traffic-routing and system-configuration information, and information about traffic to and from customer properties. For EU/EEA/UK users, it describes itself as controller for some information and processor for Customer Logs, administrator audit logs, some account settings, and content transiting its network. Its public policy says information may be accessed/transferred globally; the [Cloudflare DPA](https://www.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-customer-dpa/) becomes part of the customer agreement only when agreed. Do not infer that a particular DPA, transfer mechanism, region, or retention setting applies to this account. [Cloudflare Privacy Policy](https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/)
- Cloudflare distinguishes edge analytics on proxied traffic from its browser-based RUM/Web Analytics beacon. Edge analytics reflect requests handled by Cloudflare; the browser beacon is a separate client-side integration. For the manual RUM beacon, Cloudflare says it receives the source IP as ordinary HTTP request data, discards it at the nearest Cloudflare data centre, and does not store it in core databases or logs; the script does not access browser cookies/storage and reports performance metrics. Its FAQ says unsampled beacon data is retained for seven days, then aggregated to about 10% for long-term storage, and that Web Analytics currently does not log query strings. [Cloudflare Analytics FAQ](https://developers.cloudflare.com/analytics/faq/about-analytics/), [RUM beacon documentation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/speed/observatory/rum-beacon/), [Web Analytics FAQ](https://developers.cloudflare.com/web-analytics/faq/)
- A new configuration concern: Cloudflare’s 19 November 2026 RUM documentation says the beacon may be manually added or automatically injected into proxied pages. It states that free customers have RUM enabled automatically, with EEA/UK/Swiss traffic excluded by default, and can switch it off; other plans may enable it. This makes the live zone plan/settings and production HTML/network requests important: a manually consent-gated script does not prove automatic injection is off. [RUM beacon documentation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/speed/observatory/rum-beacon/)
- Cloudflare’s security cookies are feature-specific: for example, its documentation associates `__cf_bm` with Bot Management/Bot Fight Mode and `cf_clearance` with challenges/JavaScript detections. Their presence depends on enabled protection features, so inspect actual response headers instead of promising a fixed cookie list. [Cloudflare Cookies](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/reference/policies-compliances/cloudflare-cookies/)

### JetHost shared hosting and AWStats

- JetHost’s current [privacy policy](https://jethost.bg/policies/) says it is controller for customer account information and processor for personal data customers process using its services. It separately describes personal data it handles for the customer account and states that it does not transfer customer data to third countries. Treat this as JetHost’s public policy statement; obtain the applicable hosting terms, processing agreement, subprocessor and location details before making account-specific transfer claims.
- JetHost’s [shared-hosting page](https://jethost.bg/web-hosting/) advertises weekly backups. Its [MCP statistics documentation](https://mcp.jethost.bg/docs/cpanel/stats.html) exposes daily AWStats statistics and says its API returns up to 45 days of daily history; that is not evidence of raw-log or AWStats retention. cPanel’s [AWStats documentation](https://docs.cpanel.net/cpanel/metrics/awstats/) describes visitor traffic reports, while its [Visitors documentation](https://docs.cpanel.net/cpanel/metrics/visitors/) lists possible request-log fields such as visitor IP, URL, time, referrer and user agent. Actual fields and rotation depend on server/account settings.

### JetBackup 5

- JetBackup explicitly says it does not host backup storage: backups reside at the destination supplied by the service provider. Its documentation makes job schedules and backup-retain counts configurable; marked backups are removed when the cleanup process runs. Therefore, software documentation cannot establish this account’s backup destination, cadence, retention, deletion timing, or access controls. Confirm those with JetHost/account settings. [JetBackup 5 User Panel](https://docs.jetbackup.com/v5.4/userpanel/), [Backup Job schedules and retention](https://docs.jetbackup.com/v5.4/adminpanel/backupJobs.html)

### Tuta Mail

- Tuta says its servers are in Germany, mailbox content is encrypted, and the sender address, recipient address and email date remain readable metadata. It says it does not log login/send IPs by default and strips sent/received message IP addresses; an optional closed-session feature stores IP addresses encrypted for one week. These are Tuta’s published service claims, not verification of the mailbox account or settings. [Tuta Security and Privacy support](https://tuta.com/support/security), [Tuta encryption details](https://tuta.com/encryption)
- Tuta says a GDPR data-processing agreement is available in Settings → Subscription after registration of a business account. Confirm the actual account type and agreement before identifying Tuta as the site operator’s contracted processor. [Tuta General Support](https://tuta.com/support/general)

## General-Legal/legal-templates drafting approach

- The [General-Legal/legal-templates repository](https://github.com/General-Legal/legal-templates) describes its templates as attorney-drafted, CC0, and supplied as Markdown plus original Word files; `<mark>` text identifies fields to customise. Its GDPR-enhanced privacy-policy template is explicitly titled for a **U.S. company without an EEA/UK establishment** and includes U.S. state-law notices and broad example categories. That template is not a direct fit for a Bulgarian-established individual. Use it only as a topic/structure checklist, remove inapplicable U.S./UK content, and replace every placeholder and generic example with verified site facts. [Template README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/General-Legal/legal-templates/main/templates/privacy-policy-gdpr/README.md), [template text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/General-Legal/legal-templates/main/templates/privacy-policy-gdpr/template.md)
- The repository’s cookie-notice template offers a vendor/category/control table and covers similar device technologies, but includes generic ad-tech, mobile, and session-replay examples. Retain only what actual browser inspection confirms; use Bulgarian/EU sources for the legal rule. The repository asks users to keep its credit footnote, although its CC0 README says attribution is not legally required. [Cookie notice README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/General-Legal/legal-templates/main/templates/cookie-notice/README.md), [template text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/General-Legal/legal-templates/main/templates/cookie-notice/template.md)
